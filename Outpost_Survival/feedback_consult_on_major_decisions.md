Ask before changing how an existing feature *behaves* for the player - not just before destructive/hard-to-reverse git actions.

Said explicitly mid-task while implementing the vague "Building click UI" [[Actionable Ideas]] note, right as Claude was about to change House's click-to-upgrade from an instant blind spend into a panel-with-confirm-button (matching TrainingGroundPanel's existing pattern) - a real interaction-model change, not just added info display.

**Why:** a short/vague Actionable Ideas note (e.g. "show ... upgrade cost and button") can imply a design decision (changing an established one-click action into a two-step confirm flow) well beyond what the literal words require. The user wants a chance to weigh in on that kind of call before it's built, even though it isn't destructive or hard to revert.

**How to apply:** while executing an Actionable Ideas item (or any semi-autonomous backlog work), distinguish purely additive changes (new info shown, nothing existing removed/changed) from changes to how an existing control/flow behaves. Ship the former without asking. For the latter - even a small one, like turning a single click into click-then-confirm, or moving previously-always-visible info (e.g. resource capacity/rate) behind a hover-only tooltip - confirm the specific change with the user before implementing it, rather than picking the interpretation that seems most complete/consistent and building it.
